# Consequence Language — Auto Repair Service Advisor

This file provides plain-language consequence frameworks for
common deferred repairs. Each entry follows the same structure:

- **What it is** — one sentence, no jargon
- **What happens short term** — weeks to a few months
- **What happens long term** — months to a year or more
- **What happens if ignored completely** — worst realistic case
- **Honest urgency rating** — Address Now / Monitor Closely /
  Can Wait / Inform and Defer

Use the urgency rating to guide prioritization, not pressure.
If something is "Can Wait," say so out loud.

---

## Drivetrain & Axles

### CV Axle Boot — Cracked or Torn
**What it is:** A rubber boot that keeps grease in and dirt
out of the CV joint — the joint that lets your axle flex
while you steer.

**Short term:** Centrifugal force pushes grease out through
the crack. Joint begins running with reduced lubrication.
Customer may notice a clicking sound during turns as the
joint wears dry.

**Long term:** Joint wear accelerates. Clicking becomes
consistent. Joint begins to feel rough or notchy through
the steering wheel under load.

**If ignored:** Joint fails. In a gradual failure the axle
shaft becomes unable to transmit power smoothly. In a sudden
failure the shaft can separate or disintegrate, with debris
capable of contacting brake lines, ABS wiring, tie rods, and
other components at the knuckle. Towing required.

**Honest urgency:** Monitor Closely — the timeline depends
on how fast grease is escaping and how the customer drives.
If grease is already visibly flung around the boot area,
Address Now.

---

### CV Axle Shaft — Clicking Under Load
**What it is:** The shaft that transmits power from your
transmission to your wheels. Clicking under turns means the
joint at one end is worn past its tolerance.

**Short term:** Clicking increases in frequency and volume.
Most pronounced in slow tight turns under acceleration.

**Long term:** Joint wear becomes severe. Vibration felt
through the floor under acceleration. Power delivery becomes
inconsistent.

**If ignored:** Shaft failure. See CV Boot entry above for
failure consequences. At this stage boot replacement alone
will not resolve the issue — full shaft replacement required.

**Honest urgency:** Address Now — clicking under load means
the joint is already worn, not wearing. This one does not
improve on its own.

## Belts & Hoses

### Serpentine Belt — Cracking or Glazing
**What it is:** The belt that drives your alternator, power
steering pump, water pump, and AC compressor off the engine.

**Short term:** Cracks deepen with each heat cycle. Belt
may squeal under cold start or heavy accessory load as it
slips slightly.

**Long term:** Cracking becomes severe. Belt begins to fray
at the ribs. Slipping increases.

**If ignored:** Belt snaps without warning. Depending on the
vehicle this means immediate loss of power steering, loss of
battery charging, loss of cooling if the water pump is belt
driven, and potential engine shutdown as the battery drains
under load with no alternator input. Failure can happen at
highway speed.

**Honest urgency:** Address Now — cracked belts do not have
a predictable remaining life. Heat cycles alone can turn a
stable crack into a failure within days.

---

### Serpentine Belt Tensioner — Worn or Noisy
**What it is:** The spring-loaded pulley that keeps the
serpentine belt under correct tension at all times.

**Short term:** Belt begins to slip intermittently under
load. Squealing on startup or when AC compressor engages.

**Long term:** Inconsistent tension accelerates belt wear.
Belt may ride off the pulley under hard acceleration or
sharp turns.

**If ignored:** Tensioner seizes or collapses. Belt slips
completely off its path. Same failure consequences as belt
snapping. If replacing the belt, inspect the tensioner —
they wear together.

**Honest urgency:** Address Now if tensioner is noisy or
visibly wobbling. Monitor Closely if it is stiff but quiet.

---

### Coolant Hose — Soft, Swollen, or Cracked
**What it is:** The hoses that carry coolant between the
engine, radiator, and heater core. They keep the engine
from overheating.

**Short term:** Soft or swollen hoses are degrading from
the inside. May hold pressure fine for weeks or months.

**Long term:** Internal degradation progresses. Hose wall
thins. Exterior cracking follows internal breakdown.

**If ignored:** Hose ruptures under pressure. Coolant loss
is rapid. Engine overheats within minutes. Continued
operation after overheat risks warped cylinder head, blown
head gasket, or seized engine — repairs that cost many
times the hose replacement.

**Honest urgency:** Monitor Closely if soft but intact.
Address Now if cracked, swollen at the ends, or if the
vehicle has a history of overheating.

## Brakes

### Brake Pads — At or Near Minimum Thickness
**What it is:** The friction material that clamps against
your rotors to stop the car. Minimum thickness is typically
2-3mm depending on manufacturer spec.

**Short term:** Wear indicators (metal tabs built into the
pad) begin to contact the rotor. Squealing on braking.
This is the system working as designed — the noise is
intentional.

**Long term:** Pads wear through completely. Metal backing
plate contacts rotor directly. Squealing becomes grinding.
Rotor surface begins to score and groove.

**If ignored:** Rotors are destroyed. What was a pad
replacement becomes a pad and rotor replacement. Braking
distance increases. In extreme cases, pad material
disintegrates and caliper contacts rotor, causing brake
fade and potential loss of braking on that corner.

**Honest urgency:** Address Now at 2mm or below. Monitor
Closely at 3-4mm with a defined return date.

---

### Brake Rotors — Worn Below Minimum, Scored, or Warped
**What it is:** The metal discs your brake pads clamp
against. They have a minimum thickness specification —
below that, they cannot safely dissipate braking heat.

**Short term:** Light scoring causes slight vibration or
pulsing under braking. Warped rotors cause steering wheel
shudder when braking from highway speeds.

**Long term:** Heat management becomes poor. Brake fade
increases under repeated heavy braking. Pulsing worsens.

**If ignored:** Rotor cracks under thermal stress. Braking
becomes severely compromised. A cracked rotor is a
structural failure — not a wear item at that stage.

**Honest urgency:** Address Now if below minimum thickness
or visibly cracked. Address Now if scoring is deep enough
to catch a fingernail. Monitor Closely for light surface
rust or minor scoring on infrequently driven vehicles.

---

### Brake Fluid — Dark, Contaminated, or Past Interval
**What it is:** The hydraulic fluid that transfers your
pedal input to the brakes at each wheel. It absorbs
moisture over time, which lowers its boiling point.

**Short term:** Contaminated fluid performs adequately
under normal driving. May show slightly spongy pedal feel.

**Long term:** Moisture content rises. Boiling point drops.
Under hard or repeated braking — mountain driving, towing,
emergency stops — fluid can boil inside the lines, causing
brake fade or a sudden loss of pedal.

**If ignored:** Fluid becomes corrosive to brake system
internals. Caliper pistons, master cylinder, and ABS
components begin to corrode from the inside. Replacement
of the fluid is measured in tens of dollars. Replacement
of the components it destroys is measured in hundreds
to thousands.

**Honest urgency:** Address Now if fluid is visibly dark
or contaminated. Most manufacturers recommend replacement
every 2 years regardless of appearance.

## Steering & Suspension

### Tie Rod End — Worn or Loose
**What it is:** The linkage that connects your steering
rack to your wheel. It translates steering wheel input
into wheel movement.

**Short term:** Loose tie rod causes vague or wandering
steering. Vehicle requires more correction input to hold
a lane. Uneven tire wear develops.

**Long term:** Play in the joint increases. Steering feel
becomes noticeably imprecise. Alignment cannot be properly
set with worn tie rods — adjustments will not hold.

**If ignored:** Tie rod separates. Steering input is no
longer transmitted to the wheel. Vehicle cannot be
controlled directionally. This is a loss-of-control
failure, not a degraded performance failure.

**Honest urgency:** Address Now if significant play is
detected. Monitor Closely if wear is early and play is
minimal. Never defer if the vehicle is used for highway
driving regularly.

---

### Ball Joint — Worn
**What it is:** The pivot point that connects the wheel
hub to the suspension. It allows the wheel to move with
the road while staying connected to the vehicle.

**Short term:** Slight clunking over bumps. Minor
wandering in steering. Early wear may be difficult for
the customer to feel.

**Long term:** Clunking becomes consistent. Tire wear
accelerates on the affected corner. Vehicle may pull
slightly.

**If ignored:** Ball joint separates. The wheel collapses
inward or outward. The vehicle drops onto the rotor and
tire. Control is lost instantly. A separated ball joint
at speed is one of the most dangerous mechanical failures
a passenger vehicle can experience.

**Honest urgency:** Address Now if play is confirmed
during inspection. No ball joint failure is acceptable
to defer past a defined repair date with the customer's
full understanding of the risk.

---

### Sway Bar End Link — Worn or Broken
**What it is:** The link that connects your sway bar to
the suspension at each corner. The sway bar reduces body
roll in turns by connecting left and right suspension.

**Short term:** Clunking or rattling over bumps, speed
bumps, or uneven pavement. Most noticeable at low speeds.

**Long term:** Clunking intensifies. In a complete failure
the sway bar disconnects from one side and body roll in
turns increases noticeably.

**If ignored:** Handling becomes less predictable in
emergency maneuvers. Not a loss-of-control failure in
the way a ball joint is, but significantly degrades the
vehicle's ability to handle sudden directional changes.

**Honest urgency:** Monitor Closely. Can Wait if the
noise is minor and the customer is aware. Address before
the next winter or high-speed driving season.

---

### Wheel Bearing — Noisy
**What it is:** The bearing that allows your wheel to
spin freely on the hub. A failing bearing generates noise
and eventually allows play in the wheel itself.

**Short term:** Humming or rumbling noise that changes
pitch with speed. May sound like road noise but is
consistent and directional — usually louder when weight
shifts onto the affected corner in turns.

**Long term:** Noise intensifies. Play develops in the
wheel. ABS sensor (often integrated into the bearing
assembly) may begin to malfunction, triggering ABS or
traction control warning lights.

**If ignored:** Bearing seizes or collapses. Wheel
locks or separates from hub. At speed this is a severe
loss-of-control event.

**Honest urgency:** Address Now if play is detected in
the wheel. Monitor Closely if noise only, no play — but
define a return date. Do not defer indefinitely.

## Engine

### Oil Leak — Minor (Valve Cover Gasket, Oil Pan Gasket)
**What it is:** Gaskets that seal the engine's oil
passages from the outside. They harden and shrink with
age, allowing seepage.

**Short term:** Small oil spots under the vehicle.
Slight oil smell from the engine bay as oil contacts
hot engine surfaces. Oil level drops slowly between
changes.

**Long term:** Seepage increases as gaskets deteriorate
further. Oil contacts belts or hoses, accelerating their
wear. If oil contacts an oxygen sensor or catalytic
converter, additional issues develop.

**If ignored:** Oil level drops to the point of engine
damage if the customer does not monitor and top off
regularly. Most customers do not monitor oil between
changes.

**Honest urgency:** Monitor Closely for minor seepage.
Address Now if leak is active and dripping, if oil
is contacting belts or electrical components, or if
oil consumption between changes requires adding more
than a quart.

---

### Coolant Leak — Minor
**What it is:** Loss of coolant from any point in the
cooling system — hoses, water pump, radiator, or
reservoir.

**Short term:** Coolant level drops. Sweet smell from
the engine bay. Possible white smoke from the engine
on startup if coolant is reaching hot surfaces.

**Long term:** Coolant loss continues. Reservoir runs
low. Engine begins to run warmer than normal.

**If ignored:** Engine overheats. Overheat events cause
warped heads, failed head gaskets, cracked blocks, and
seized engines. A minor coolant leak that costs under
$200 to fix can produce an engine replacement scenario
if the vehicle is driven to failure.

**Honest urgency:** Address Now. There is no safe
version of an active coolant leak on a vehicle being
driven regularly.

---

### Air Filter — Heavily Clogged
**What it is:** The filter that keeps dirt and debris
out of the engine's air intake. A clogged filter
restricts airflow to the combustion chamber.

**Short term:** Slight reduction in fuel efficiency.
Minor decrease in throttle response, most noticeable
at highway speeds or under load.

**Long term:** Restriction increases. Engine works
harder for the same output. Fuel economy drops
measurably.

**If ignored:** In severe cases, a collapsed filter
can allow debris into the intake. More commonly, the
customer simply overpays for fuel for months before
replacing a filter that costs under $30.

**Honest urgency:** Can Wait if only slightly dirty.
Address Now if visibly clogged or past manufacturer
interval. This is one of the few recommendations where
showing the customer the filter is almost always
immediately convincing — a black, packed filter
explains itself.

## Tires

### Tread Depth — Approaching Minimum
**What it is:** The depth of the grooves in the tire
that channel water away from the contact patch. Legal
minimum in most states is 2/32". Safe minimum for
wet weather performance is 4/32".

**Short term:** Wet weather stopping distances begin
to increase. Hydroplaning risk rises on standing water.
Dry weather performance remains largely normal.

**Long term:** Below 2/32" the tire is legally bald
in most jurisdictions. Wet braking distances are
significantly extended.

**If ignored:** Tire fails to manage water at highway
speeds. Hydroplaning becomes likely in rain. Sidewall
vulnerability to road debris increases as tread wears.

**Honest urgency:** Address Now at 2/32". Monitor
Closely at 3-4/32". Use the penny/quarter test to
show the customer — visible and immediate.

---

### Uneven Tire Wear
**What it is:** A wear pattern that differs across the
tire's width, indicating misalignment, improper
inflation, or suspension wear.

**Outer edge wear:** Alignment issue — toe or camber
out of spec. Tire is running at an angle to the road.

**Inner edge wear:** Same as above, opposite direction.

**Center wear:** Tire has been chronically overinflated.
Contact patch is reduced to the center of the tread.

**Edge wear both sides:** Tire has been chronically
underinflated. Sidewalls are carrying load they are
not designed for.

**Cupping or scalloping:** Shock absorber or strut
wear. Tire is bouncing rather than maintaining contact
with the road surface.

**Why it matters:** Uneven wear shortens tire life
significantly. More importantly, the cause of the wear
is usually a suspension or alignment issue that will
destroy the next set of tires at the same rate if left
uncorrected.

**Honest urgency:** Show the customer the wear pattern
before recommending tires. A customer who sees the wear
understands why the alignment or suspension conversation
matters. A customer who is just told they need an
alignment after buying tires feels sold.

## Fluids

### Power Steering Fluid — Dark or Low
**What it is:** The hydraulic fluid that assists
steering effort on vehicles with hydraulic power steering.

**Short term:** Low fluid causes whining or groaning
when turning, especially at low speeds or on full lock.

**Long term:** Continued low fluid causes pump wear.
Dark, contaminated fluid indicates internal component
breakdown — metal particles circulating through the
system.

**If ignored:** Power steering pump fails. Steering
becomes extremely heavy — manageable for most drivers
but dangerous in emergency situations. Pump replacement
is significantly more expensive than fluid service.

**Honest urgency:** Address Now if low and pump is
whining. Monitor Closely if slightly dark. Dark and
metallic — Address Now and inspect the pump.

---

### Transmission Fluid — Dark, Burnt Smell, or Low
**What it is:** The fluid that lubricates, cools, and
hydraulically operates the automatic or CVT transmission.

**Short term:** Slightly dark fluid with no smell is
normal wear. Burnt smell indicates the fluid has been
overheated.

**Long term:** Degraded fluid loses its ability to
protect internal components. Shift quality deteriorates.
Hesitation or slipping becomes noticeable.

**If ignored:** Transmission internal components wear
without adequate lubrication. Clutch packs, solenoids,
and valve body components fail. A transmission service
costs hundreds. A transmission replacement costs
thousands.

**Honest urgency:** Monitor Closely for dark fluid past
interval. Address Now for burnt smell or slipping. Note:
on high-mileage vehicles with neglected transmission
fluid, a service can sometimes trigger slipping as the
fresh fluid removes debris that was providing friction.
Be honest about this risk with the customer before
proceeding.

---

### Differential Fluid — Past Interval or Low
**What it is:** The gear oil that lubricates the
differential — the component that allows your wheels
to rotate at different speeds in turns.

**Short term:** Low or degraded fluid causes slight
whining from the differential under load, most
noticeable at highway speeds.

**Long term:** Gear wear accelerates. Whining becomes
consistent. Heat generation in the differential
increases.

**If ignored:** Differential gears wear to failure.
Replacement or rebuild is significantly more expensive
than a fluid change. On 4WD vehicles, neglected
transfer case fluid produces the same progression.

**Honest urgency:** Can Wait if slightly past interval
with no symptoms. Monitor Closely with minor noise.
Address Now with consistent whining under load.
